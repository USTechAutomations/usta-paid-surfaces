# hazmat-ship-pack — mission

**What it sells.** One private web page for one UN identification number: every
row the federal Hazardous Materials Table (49 CFR 172.101) prints for it, the
four-part description sequence § 172.202 asks for laid out as a worksheet line,
label artwork proofs drawn to the measurements in § 172.407, the column 8A
exception sections explained one at a time, the 8B and 8C packaging sections with
their headings and opening paragraph, the quantity and vessel-stowage columns
with a glossary, and the column 7 special provisions in the rule's own words.

**Who buys it.** A small shipper or an e-commerce seller with one dangerous good
to send by road in the United States — a lithium battery, an aerosol, a tin of
paint, a bottle of perfume, hand sanitiser, dry ice. They searched for something
like "UN1993 shipping requirements" and found the free page for that number.

**Price and delivery.** $49, one payment, not a subscription. A private page
within 15 minutes of payment. If it is not there, replying to the Stripe receipt
gets the address by hand. Refund on request within 14 days.

**Guardrails applied.** Road only: the IATA and IMDG rulebooks are copyrighted
and their terms forbid extraction, so they are never quoted, summarised or
paraphrased — the page says so in place of covering them. Labels are drawn from
the regulation's stated dimensions and are marked artwork proofs, never compliant
labels; no published pictogram is copied. Nothing on any page, free or paid,
tells a buyer what their shipment is, needs or is excused from — a trained
shipper classifies the material and signs the certification, and the pages repeat
that wherever a reader might forget it. The free estate keeps the table row and
the column glossary; the exceptions and the packaging walk-through are the paid
part.
