# MISSION — ai-disclosure-notice

## What it sells

The wording a company puts in front of people when a machine, and not a person,
is doing the talking, the writing or the deciding. Eight ready notices: a chat
banner, a first-message line, a label for generated text, a label for synthetic
pictures and audio, a companion-chatbot line, a line for a selling bot, and a
note for a decision made without a human in the loop. Each comes as plain text
anyone can paste, and as page code a developer drops into a site.

## Who buys it

The person at a small software company who has just been asked "do we need to
say this is AI?" and has no lawyer to ask. They do not want to write it, and
they do not want to guess which of seven different laws is behind it.

## Price and delivery

**$49, paid once.** Not a subscription. Payment opens a private page within
about fifteen minutes, carrying every notice with the buyer's own organisation
name filled in, the pasteable code, a checklist of where each notice goes, and
the dated table of rules behind them.

## Free, so anyone can check the work first

The whole rules table and the whole notice generator run free, in the browser,
with no sign-up. The free drafts carry a watermark. The rules table does not:
anybody may read which law says what, in the exact quoted words, linked to the
government page they came from.

## Guardrails

No page tells a reader what their own legal position is. Rows say whether they
match the answers typed in, never whether a law "applies to you". Nothing is
hosted for the buyer, so there is no page to keep alive and no badge to police.
Answers stay in the buyer's browser tab and are never sent anywhere.
