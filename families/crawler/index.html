A name disappearing is not the same as a block coming off, and that is the one people most often get wrong: when a crawler is not named, whatever the file says under User-agent: * applies to it instead.

The size of the panel, and the honest count

We ask 100,000 sites for their robots.txt, and we hold a dated copy of the panel from 71 of the 77 days since 9 Jun 2026. 6 days are missing — a 2-day stretch from 11 Jun to 12 Jun, 19 Jun, 4 Jul, 15 Jul and 8 Aug 2026. We hold no copy from those days and no record of a run on them either. We are not going to invent a reason for that, and nothing on this page counts those days as read. On our newest read, 24 Aug 2026, 61,439 of them gave us a file we could save, 54,342 of those were a real robots.txt and not one of the 4,717 web pages or 2,380 plain files with no User-agent line sent in their place, and 14,627 of the real ones give at least one AI crawler a User-agent line of its own. Across the 8 reads this page counts, 17 Aug 2026 to 24 Aug 2026, 363 sites changed what their file says about a named AI crawler, across 3,144 crawler-level changes.

Every number in that paragraph was counted out of the stored files themselves when this page was built, and each one is shown again, broken down, on what is and is not in this feed. Ask us and we will show the working.

2,619 file changes on 719 sites were thrown out of this count on purpose. Those files swing between an old and a new version day to day, which is two of the site’s servers answering differently, not the site changing its mind. Counting them would inflate the number and every one of them would be a false alarm in your inbox.

17,003 more file changes were left out because one of the two days answered with something we cannot read as a policy: a web page or a bot-block screen in 16,741 of them, and plain text with no User-agent line in the other 262. Neither is a policy and we do not read either as one.

A very big file is saved up to a limit and no further. The limit is 512 KB. 108,788 of the 1,231,774 copies we hold are cut that way, and 24 changes in this window touched one, so we left those out rather than guess at the part we never read.

548 sites answered us on 24 Aug 2026 and left us nothing to keep, and 5,729 refused us outright. We do not know what any of their files said that day, and we do not guess.

Doing this yourself

You can fetch any site’s robots.txt yourself in a second. What you cannot do is fetch yesterday’s. Nothing about that file is versioned, announced or archived.

To watch a panel this size you would have to fetch 100,000 files every day, store every copy, and then work out which differences are a real policy change and which are two of the site’s servers disagreeing. That last part is most of the work: it is the 2,619 file changes above that we throw away.
