Why every row carries two dates

The right-hand date is the register’s, and it is not a joining date. It moves forward every time the seller ships a new version, so a server that has been listed since June can print yesterday’s date. The only way to know when a name actually arrived is to have held the list on the day before.

The left-hand date is ours, and it is a fact about our copies, not about the seller. Where the two are more than a day apart we print the reason next to the row: either we hold no copy for those days, or our copy that day stopped at the first page. We would rather say that than let you assume we were watching on a day we were not.

The size of what we hold, and the honest count

We hold 29 dated copies of this register, one a day, from 22 Jul 2026 to 22 Aug 2026, and 3,036 dated rows inside them — one row per name per copy. Our first copy held 95 names and our copy of 22 Aug 2026 held 128. Across all 29 copies, 129 different names have been on the list at some point. 34 are in the newest copy and not in the first. One went the other way. We also caught 41 version changes on 19 servers, which is the only other thing the register will tell you about who has been shipping.

Every copy from that day on is the whole list, and both ends of the comparison above are whole copies — but on those 17 days a name could have been on the register and missing from our copy. 14 names disappear from a copy in the middle of their own run and come back later, and every one of those gaps falls on one of those days.

One name has left the register while we were watching, and that means the register stopped listing it. It does not mean the company shut down or the server was switched off. We only know what the list said on the day.

We publish no score, no grade and no ranking of any named company, and we do not work one out. Three of the names in our copies sell exactly that, and they are kept off the tables on these pages for that reason. They are still inside every count above.

The register only tells us what a seller filed. We do not call the address to see whether anything answers. 49 of the 128 names on our newest copy give no address to call at all, and 36 of those 49 give no website either. 73 of the 128 do give a website. Every one of those is what the seller typed into the register, not something we checked.

Doing this yourself

You can read the register yourself in a second. What you cannot do is read yesterday’s. It is not versioned, not announced and not archived, and the date it shows against a server is the date of that server’s newest version, not the date it joined.

To answer “who shipped this month” you would have had to start saving the whole list a month ago, keep every copy, and then work out which differences are a real arrival and which are your own reader stopping short. That last part is most of the work, and it is the part we print rather than hide.
