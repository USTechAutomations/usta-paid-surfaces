We refuse to build a row out of a page fingerprint. A fingerprint is a short code that changes if anything on the page changes — a rotating quote, a number in a script tag. Of the 309 company-and-page pairs we hold 60 or more copies of, 87 produce a brand new fingerprint on every single copy. Selling those as hiring changes would put a false alarm in your inbox almost every day, so no count on this feed is a count of fingerprints.

181 of the 320 page copies we stored on 22 Aug 2026 are cut short. They hit our 256 KiB size cap, so the end of the page is not in the copy. Cut is not the same as useless: 101 of the 174 copies we can read a list out of are themselves cut, because the block of data holding the list closed before the cut. What we will not do is read a list out of a block that did not close — if the cut lands inside the list we read nothing rather than a short list. The one risk we cannot rule out is a company that splits its roles across two blocks and loses the second one to the cut.

Why so few of them can be read

Most careers pages today are an empty shell that fetches the jobs afterwards from a hiring tool — Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday and the rest. The page we save is the shell. The roles were never in it, so there is nothing in our copy to read, and no amount of care on our side changes that.

The three that work publish their own list inside the page itself, as data the page carries. We read that list, follow each role by the id the company gave it, and compare two dated copies. Not one role on these three pages changed id between two copies, which is why an appearance on this feed is a real appearance and not the whole list being rebuilt.

What would widen this is not more companies. It is the 116 companies whose page we store but cut short. Raising the cap and copying again would tell us whether their list of roles was past the cut, or was never in the page at all. We do not know which today and we will not guess. Until that is done and counted, this feed is three companies and says so on every page.

Doing this yourself

You can open any company’s jobs page in a second. What you cannot open is last month’s. Nothing about that page is versioned, announced or archived, and a role that was up in June leaves no trace once it comes down.

To hold what we hold you would have to fetch two pages from every company on your list every day, store every copy, and then work out which differences are a real move and which are the page shuffling itself. That last part is most of the work, and it is the part that makes the numbers wrong when it is skipped.
