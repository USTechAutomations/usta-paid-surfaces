What a boatyard haul-out plan has to get right

Booking two hundred haul-outs is a constraint problem wearing a telephone. The lift has a weight limit and a sling width, the day has a limit, and every hauled boat needs a patch of ground it actually fits on. Here is every rule we apply and every reason a boat comes back unplaced.

The four reasons a boat does not get a haul dateread out of the scheduler · four reasons

A season plan that quietly leaves a boat out is a broken plan, not a partial one. Every boat the scheduler cannot place comes back named, with one of these reasons beside it — never in a single mixed failure bucket, because “your lift is too small for this boat” and “you ran out of days” need completely different answers from you.

Every reason the scheduler can refuse to place a boat read out of season.py
What it meansWhy it happensThe code your file will carry
The boat weighs more than the travel lift can pick up.Its displacement is greater than the lift tonnage on your own yard record.over_lift_tons
The boat is wider than the gap between the lift’s slings.Its beam is greater than the sling spacing on your own yard record. This is a different refusal from being too heavy, and it gets a different answer.over_sling_beam
No patch of ground is big enough once the stands are counted.A spot has to take the boat’s length plus your bow-and-stern clearance AND its beam plus your stand clearance. Booking on length alone is how a yard runs out of ground halfway through the season.no_storage_spot
Every working day in the season is already full.Days are counted against the hauls-per-day figure on your yard record, after weekends you do not work and days you closed by name have been taken out.no_day_capacity

What is deliberately not built

  • We do not send anything to a boat ownerOwner letters are drafted and handed to a gate that refuses them and keeps the copy. One attempt has been made and one was refused, the last one because there is no approval file for yard-season/email. No owner has ever heard from us.
  • We never touch an owner’s depositThe haul-out deposit an owner pays goes into the yard’s own account. We never hold it, route it or see it, and the code refuses any destination other than the yard by name rather than by convention. Holding other people’s deposits is money transmission, and it would put your payout account at risk of a chargeback that has nothing to do with us.
  • We never see a cardNo payment details of any kind reach us at any point.
  • Your prices are yours, and they are stored per yardThere is no shared rate anywhere in this product. Two yards with identical numbers still get their own rows, so nothing we change for one yard can reach another. A work order is held, not printed with a figure we invented, if the rate it needs is missing.
  • One refusal code is declared and never usedThe second reading accepts after_season_end as a reason, and nothing in the scheduler produces it. It is written here rather than left off, because a list of reasons is only useful if it is the whole list. This page prints the ones that can actually happen.
