Doing this yourself

Every ISO publishes today’s queue and overwrites the last one. To get what moved you have to download each queue on the same day every week, keep the old copy somewhere you can still read it, and line up 6,691 projects across four different file layouts.

Miss one week and that week is gone. The ISO does not republish it, and there is no public archive to go back to. That is the whole reason this file exists.

How it works

  1. You email us and we send a checkout link in that thread.
  2. You reply with the queues that matter to you, and we say what we can and cannot collect for them.
  3. Each week a person emails you the what-moved file, and names anything we could not collect.

What this page cannot tell you

A move is two copies disagreeing, and nothing more. A row here means the same public queue file said one thing on one dated copy and something else on the next. It is not an approval, a rejection, a decision or a filing. If you need to know why a project moved, the operator is the place to ask, and we will say so rather than guess for you.

Two of the queues we read have stopped coming. Our last MISO file is 6 Aug 2026 and our last ERCOT file is 30 Jul 2026. Every page built on those two says “last copy” and promises you nothing further. The weekly comparison covers the four queues that are still arriving.

The names and the fuels are the operators’ own words. Each operator writes the same fuel differently — Solar, SOL, SUN and S all turn up for the same thing — and SPP publishes no plant name at all, only its own queue number. We print what they printed and mark a label we are not sure of as not clear, rather than tidying it into a category we invented.

This feed came off sale on 24 Aug 2026

There is no price on this page and no card button, and that is not a technical problem. The Southwest Power Pool is one of the four queues this feed reads, and their own terms page grants permission to copy and pass on their material with a proper credit — except inside a commercial publication. Questions people ask

This data is public and free. What is this page for, then?

The data is not the hard part. Last week’s copy of it is.

Every ISO publishes today’s queue and overwrites the last one. Fetch CAISO now and you get today. There is nowhere to fetch last Tuesday. We have been keeping a dated copy since 11 Jun 2026 — weekly at first, then one every day since 9 Aug 2026, 25 dated copies in all — so the question “what moved” still has an answer. What this page shows is built out of those copies. If you already keep your own dated copies, you do not need us.

Are you allowed to collect this?

Everything here comes from a public queue file we fetched with a collector that says who it is.

Where a source says no, we stop, and we stop on the day we find out. MISO only serves a browser, and the only way to fetch it was to pretend to be one, so we read it ten times, the last on 6 Aug 2026, and stopped. ERCOT’s own robots file disallows the two paths its queue sits under, so we read it nine times, the last on 30 Jul 2026, and stopped. What we sealed before those days is real and we still hold it, and nothing newer comes in from either. Both are named on this page instead of quietly missing. We could get around either one and we are not going to.

You are a small company. What happens if you stop?

Two answers, and neither of them is a promise that we will not.

Nothing here is behind a login, so nothing here disappears with an account. And we are not going to tell you we have a certification or a famous client list, because we do not have either.

How do I know these rows are real?

Check one. Take Menifee Power Bank from the table above and look it up in the CAISO public queue. Every project on this page is a real project in a public file, and both dates are the days we sealed our copies.

The rows we could not stand behind are not here. When a read comes back an error page instead of a queue file we seal nothing for that day and skip it, which is what happened to SPP on 13 Aug 2026 and to CAISO on 22 Aug 2026. A skipped day is named on the page for that queue, never quietly dropped.
