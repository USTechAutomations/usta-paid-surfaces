What we read, and what we could not

We fetched each state's own legislature or notary office and looked for the journal provision in the words on the page. 18 of 51 answered with a provision we could quote. The rest answered with an application shell, a refusal, or a page without the provision on it, and they say so on their own page rather than carrying a filled-in answer.

Of the ones we could read, 11 carry words permitting a journal in an electronic format. 1 describe a bound paper journal with no such words, 0 tie an electronic journal to a provider the state approves, and 39 are unsettled.

How the tool keeps a record honest

  • Locked before it is storedA passphrase you type becomes a key inside the browser, 200,000 rounds of PBKDF2, and the entries are sealed with AES-GCM. A wrong passphrase opens nothing.
  • Nothing is editedEntries are added, never changed or removed. A mistake is fixed by a correction that names the earlier number, so the record shows both. Numbers are never reused.
  • A backup is not optionalA browser can clear its own storage without warning. The tool asks for a folder where it can write an encrypted backup, and where it cannot, it refuses the next entry until today's backup file has been saved.
  • A thumbprint stays on paperWhere a state's text asks for a thumbprint, the tool says so and does not take one. A thumbprint has to be taken on paper and kept with a paper journal.
