What Europe's new machinery rules say about your manual

A US machine builder ships into Europe. From a fixed date, the instructions and the safety information have to be in a language the people using the machine can easily understand, and the country you are selling into is the one that decides which language that is. Here are the dates and the article numbers, so you can go and read it yourself.

The date that matters is the day a machine is PLACED on the market, not the day it was built and not the day it was sold on. A machine placed on the market before that date under the old rules can still be made available afterwards. That is the difference between having a deadline and not having one, and it is why anybody telling you everything you have already built is about to become illegal is wrong.

The 5 things this page will not tell you

Written down here rather than left for you to find out the hard way. Each one is something we could say that would make this feel more urgent than it is, and each one is something we cannot point at a sentence for.

  • “the fine is X”penalties are set by each country, not by the Regulation. Article 50 hands it to the Member States. We have read none of the 27 national rules, so we do not have a number and will not invent one. (Article 50(1))
  • “your country needs language Y”each Member State determines the language. Our country-to-language list is the official-language reference data, which is not the same thing as a determination and is marked as reference data everywhere it appears. (Article 10(7))
  • “you are non-compliant today”as of 2026-08-24 the Regulation does not apply yet. It applies from 20 January 2027. This product sells getting ready, not a compliance verdict on today. (Article 54)
  • “everything already built is now illegal”machines placed on the market before 20 January 2027 in conformity with the old directive can still be made available afterwards. The clock is on PLACING on the market. (Article 52(1))
  • “we can sign your declaration”it is the manufacturer's statement under the manufacturer's sole responsibility. There is no version of this product that signs one. (Annex V, Part A, point 4)

Every one of those numbers has to survive into every language version, and a number is compared by what it is worth, never by how it is spelt. German writes four and a half as 4,5 where English writes 4.5, and writes fifteen hundred as 1.500 where English writes 1,500. A checker that compared the spelling would shout at every correct translation and then go quiet on the one where 4.5 had become 45. That is the whole reason this exists.
