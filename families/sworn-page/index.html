What we are allowed to read, and on whose say-soa dated decision, 2026-08-25

A lane may fetch a company's PUBLIC pages (its robots file, its public product or service pages) to fill a row on one of our comparison tables, provided ALL of these hold:

  • The host's robots file does not forbid us, and does not name and refuse AI crawlers (engine/fetch.py treats a host that refuses named AI agents as refusing us).
  • A per-host permission note exists under permissions/ carrying the robots fingerprint and the date it was read (tools/review_sources.py writes it). No note = UNKNOWN = no fetch.
  • The host's written terms, where a terms page is found, do not forbid automated reading. Terms not found = "terms: not separately reviewed", recorded as such, never as a yes.
  • Pacing: never more than one request every 5 seconds per host.
  • What is read is used to state FACTS about a company (what its robots file says, what its public page states), never to contact it and never to republish its text.

And the same decision’s own list of what it does not allow:

  • Contacting anyone. Sending anything. Signing up for anything. Spending anything.
  • Reading pages about a private individual (F6 stays in force).
  • Republishing a publisher's document text; derived facts only.
  • Self-granting: a machine may write a permission NOTE from evidence, never a permission DECISION. The decision is this file.

Those lists are that decision’s own words, read out of it as this page was built, and the decision is dated 2026-08-25. They are not a summary of it. If the decision is ever withdrawn, this page stops being built the same day — it is checked on every build, and a page that told you we had permission we no longer had is the one thing this table cannot be.

The file names a few of our own folders and our own code in passing. Those are ours, and they are left in because taking them out would make this a tidied-up summary of a decision instead of the decision.
