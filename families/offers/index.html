11 open offers. Each one is a small piece of automation we will build for a named kind of business, at a fixed price, inside a stated number of days. Prices run from $200 to $450, and every offer states its own delivery window: the shortest is 3 days and the longest 12, counted from the day the scope is agreed rather than the day you pay.

8 of the 11 are paid once. The other 3 are priced per month. Read the price line on the offer itself before you write to us: this page used to say all eleven were one-time builds, and that was wrong.

Each offer says who it is for, the problem we think that business has, what we would ask before starting, and how long the build takes.

None of them is a finished product. The page says so itself. They are offers we are testing: if you want one, we build it to the scope stated. We would rather you know that before you write to us.

The evidence pages

Three pages exist so you can check what we claim without taking a sales line on trust. They are public and they are meant to be read by someone trying to catch us out.

  • Verification certificatesEach one shows its signed record, the exact text that was signed, the signature, the public key, and the tool you use to check it.
  • Provenance charterWhat the signing promises, what it deliberately does not cover, and every older gap we have counted.
  • Sealed-estate catalogEvery dated series we keep, the range we hold, how often it updates, and where the gaps are.

How this differs from a feed

An offer is a build: we deliver a working thing inside an agreed window. 8 of the 11 are paid once and 3 are priced per month. A feed is a subscription: you pay monthly and a file arrives saying what moved, with no build in it.

They are different buyers and we do not bundle them. If you came here for a dated change file, go back to the feed directory.
