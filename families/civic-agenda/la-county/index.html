What this page cannot tell you

  • The meeting list and the item list come from two different Los Angeles County files and they do not line up. 1,033 of the 1,040 items we hold sit with a body that never appears on the meeting list we hold, so do not read the item count as the docket of any one body.
  • On 17 of our 62 reads, Los Angeles County returned a meeting list with nothing on it. We ask for the meetings dated in the last 14 days and ahead, and on those days Los Angeles County had none. That is a short agenda, not a failed read: the read worked every time and the answer was an empty list, not an error. Its meeting list has come back with nothing in that window on every read since 19 Aug 2026, so the newest meeting we hold is from 18 Aug 2026 while its agenda items are current to 23 Aug 2026.
  • We can only show you a change that happened between two of our reads. If a council put an agenda up and took it down again inside one day, we did not see it and we will not pretend we did. We missed 12 days: 19 Jun 2026, 4 Jul 2026, 8 Jul 2026, 15 Jul 2026 and 8 Aug 2026, plus a 7-day stretch from 11 Aug 2026 to 17 Aug 2026. Anything that moved and moved back inside one of those gaps is not in this feed, and we will not claim it is.
  • We hold eight governments and no others: Chicago, Seattle, King County, Mesa, Austin, Phoenix, Columbus, and Los Angeles County.
  • We ask Los Angeles County for the meetings dated in the last 14 days and ahead, and for the items Los Angeles County edited in the last 7 days. That window is what we hold, not the whole docket. So a row leaving one of these lists means it left this file: it does not mean the meeting was cancelled, the item was killed, or that anyone named on it went away.
  • A meeting dropping off the list is usually the list rolling forward past the meeting date, not a withdrawal. That happened 3 times here and is kept out of the table above on purpose.
