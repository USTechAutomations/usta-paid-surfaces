What we catch, and how many of each

Every kind of change below is counted from our own dated copies. Nothing is rolled into a bigger number to make it look better. One more kind, a meeting record swapped for a brand new one, is in the section after this because it needs explaining.

  • 166 agendas stamped published again at the same addressThe government’s own “last published” time moved while the web address stayed the same. Whatever was at that address before is not there now, and nothing on the public site marks it.
  • 44 agendas posted for the first timeThe meeting was already on the calendar with no agenda. Now there is one, and you know the day it went up.
  • 8 agendas moved to a different address, and 1 had the link taken downOften a supplemental agenda replacing the first one a few days before the meeting. The first address stops being the agenda and no redirect tells you.
  • 28 meetings changed statusIn the words the government uses itself — Tentative to Final, Final to Final Revised, Scheduled & Published to Cancelled. We do not translate them.
  • 227 meetings appeared on the calendarBetween one of our reads and the next. Five more were added after the meeting date had already passed.
  • 18 meetings were pulled before they happenedOff the calendar while the date was still ahead, and never back on a later list. It left this list; it does not mean the meeting was cancelled. Another 12 dropped off and returned on a later read; those are labelled as coming back, not as withdrawals.
  • 82 meetings had minutes postedThe record of what happened going up, with the day it appeared.
  • 3 meetings moved to a different date and 5 changed where they meetSmall numbers, and real ones. We would rather print three than round three up.

That is 587 change rows in all. A row can carry more than one thing that moved, so the list above adds up to slightly more than that.

What a meeting dropping off the list does not mean

Most drop-offs are not withdrawals. A meeting drops off because the published list rolls forward past the meeting date. That happened 237 times in our copies, and every one of those is kept out of the tables on this page. Selling them to you as pulled meetings would multiply the headline number by about 13 and every extra row would be a false alarm.

Only 18 meetings came off the calendar while the date was still ahead and stayed off. That is the number, and it is small because the thing itself is rare. Another 12 dropped off and were back on a later read. Those are labelled as coming back, not as withdrawals, because that is what happened.

A record going is not always the meeting going. Once, a government dropped a meeting record and put up a brand new one, with a new number, for the same body on the same day. We check for that before calling anything a withdrawal. Chicago’s Committee on Aviation meeting of 29 Jun 2026 is one of those in our copies, and reading it as a pulled meeting would have been exactly backwards.

An internal version number is not a change. These records carry a row-version field that the software moves on its own. 242 meeting records in our copies came back with a different fingerprint while every field a reader would recognise stayed exactly as it was. Not one of those is counted as a change here.

What this page cannot tell you

We record what the meeting list said, not what is inside the agenda. 19,840 of the 24,503 meeting records we hold carry a link to the agenda document itself, and we have never opened one. We can tell you an item was on the list on one dated copy and gone on the next. We cannot tell you which line inside that document changed.

A meeting dropping off the list is usually the calendar moving, not a cancellation. These lists only show a window, so a meeting leaves one by happening. The tables above keep those out on purpose rather than selling you a date passing as news.

We only see a change between two of our own reads. A council that put an agenda up and pulled it down again inside a day did something we never saw, and we will not pretend otherwise. The days we hold no copy at all are named above rather than left for you to find.

Doing this yourself

You can open any of these meeting lists right now, for free. What you cannot open is yesterday’s. The list is replaced in place, the agenda file is replaced in place, and neither keeps a history you can reach.

To get what moved you would have to read eight meeting lists every day, keep every copy, and then work out which differences are real. That last part is most of the work: these records carry a version number that moves when nothing else has, and meetings drop off the list for the ordinary reason that the date has passed. Count either of those as a change and your alerts become noise.
