Newest sealed read: 2026-08-24. We read this source every day. We hold 579 sealed runs going back to 2026-06-11.

  • We ask a source on a day, and keep exactly what it said.Word for word, in a copy that is never edited afterwards. If the source rewrites the page tomorrow, our copy of yesterday is untouched, and it is stamped with the day we took it.
  • Every copy gets a fingerprint.A fingerprint is a short code worked out from the record itself. Change any part of the record, even one character, and the code comes out completely different. So the code is a way of proving a copy has not been touched.
  • Each record gets two fingerprints, and the second one is the useful one.One covers the record together with the day we read it, so it is different every day by design. The other covers the record on its own. If that second code is identical on two different days, the source said exactly the same thing both times. If it is different, something moved, and you can see what.
  • 386 earthquakes were read on more than one day. 333 said exactly the same thing both times and 53 had changed.Counted out of the earthquake database while this page was built, by comparing those second fingerprints. That is the number the whole idea rests on: most records do not move, and the ones that do are findable.
  • We worked out 25 fingerprints again while writing this page, across 16 readers. Every one matched.One record out of each set of records we keep, rebuilt from the stored copy with the same method that sealed it. If any of them had come out different it would have been left off this page, and it would have been said out loud.
  • We have gone back to a source and got the same fingerprint out.On 13 Aug 2026 we fetched clinical study NCT00520975 from ClinicalTrials.gov again and the record came back with the same fingerprint we had sealed for it. That is the check working from the outside in, not just from our own files.

What this page cannot tell you

  • A fingerprint proves our copy has not changed. It does not prove the source was right.If a source published something wrong on the day, we sealed a faithful copy of something wrong. What we can prove is what it said and when. What it should have said is a different question and we do not answer it.
  • It does not prove we read everything, or read it at the right moment.A change that happened and was undone between two of our reads is a change we never saw. Where a reader is behind or switched off, the coverage page says so rather than this page hiding it.
  • We hold our own copy. We do not hold a signed statement from the source.If someone on the other side of an argument needs more than our word plus our fingerprints, say so before you buy and we will tell you plainly what we can and cannot give you.
